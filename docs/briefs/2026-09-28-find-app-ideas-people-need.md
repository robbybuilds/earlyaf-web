## Outline

### 1. Hook (problem)
Solo builders waste months on “cool” ideas. The bottleneck isn’t more brainstorming — it’s finding problems that already show up in the wild with consequence and recurrence.

### 2. Rule: discovery > invention
You don’t invent demand. You find repeated firsthand complaints / workarounds, then decide if you can ship a first experiment.

### 3. Where to look (methods — educational, not product-only)
- App Store / Play **1–2 star reviews** — same complaint across apps = signal  
- Reddit / founder communities — “wish there was”, “looking for a tool”, “frustrated with”  
- Workarounds (spreadsheets, Zapier glue, manual DMs) — people already paying with time  
- Search / trends interest ≠ willingness to pay (call this out; matches Early AF evidence law)

### 4. Evidence bar (from Early AF SoT — teach our standards)
- Separate observation from “you should build X”  
- Prefer independent firsthand reports; dates matter  
- Complaint alone ≠ build recommendation  
- Heuristic (label as rule of thumb, not magic): look for recurrence across conversations + concrete consequence; prefer recent signals  

### 5. Solo-builder filter
Even a real problem may not be for you: skills, time, distribution. Name the filter; Early AF recommendations factor solo feasibility without excluding useful research.

### 6. Soft product bridge (honest)
Early AF publishes a **free reviewed daily idea** and helps builders research markets before they commit. Reply Radar (listen/score/queue for Reddit/X worth-reply posts) is a separate surface — don’t fold it into this page’s promises. CTA: try the free daily idea / “Find ideas for my audience”.

### 7. FAQ (AEO)
- What’s the difference between an idea list and market research?  
- How many complaints before it’s worth investigating?  
- Is search volume enough to build? (No.)  
- How is this different from IdeaBrowser? (Catalog of ideas vs research → decision to build / reach customers; don’t trash-talk — differentiate on evidence + next experiment.)
