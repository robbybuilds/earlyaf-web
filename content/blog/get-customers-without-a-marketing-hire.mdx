You do not need a marketing hire to get your first customers. You need a few honest channels, a clear promise, and the discipline to show up by hand.

Agencies sell calendars. Growth threads sell “systems.” Solo builders who ship early usually win with **small, manual distribution** — the same rooms where they already [found the idea](/blog/how-to-find-app-ideas-people-need) and [named the audience](/blog/how-to-research-your-app-audience).

Written for **solo app builders**, not agencies.

## What “without a hire” actually means

It means you own distribution for now:

- You pick **one or two channels** you can reach without inventing an audience
- You speak in **their language**, from dated evidence — not brand voice decks
- You reply and follow up **yourself** — no auto-reply, no outreach blast, no “we’ll engage for you”
- You measure **conversations and next steps**, not vanity impressions

It does **not** mean posting into the void every day, buying followers, or pretending a listening hub will acquire users while you sleep.

## Preconditions (skip these and channels waste your week)

Before you “do marketing”:

1. A **recurring problem** with dated evidence — not one viral rant ([build signal bar](/blog/when-a-customer-complaint-is-a-build-signal)).
2. A **named audience slice** — who speaks, where, with what workaround.
3. A **one-line promise** in their words (“stops X so you can Y”).
4. Ideally, [one first marketing experiment](/blog/how-to-run-a-first-marketing-experiment-before-you-build) designed so you know pass/fail.

Channels amplify a promise. They do not invent demand.

## Honest small channels that fit a solo calendar

### 1. Manual replies in rooms they already use

Reddit, X, niche Discords, review threads — same places you research. Use a [worth-reply bar](/blog/when-a-reddit-or-x-reply-is-worth-your-time): ICP fit, specific ask, freshness, useful without pitching first. Soft next step only after you helped.

This is slow on purpose. A handful of sharp answers beat fifty template CTAs.

### 2. One promise page + waitlist shared where they hang out

A single page that names the complaint-shaped promise and collects email. Share it in threads that clear your reply bar, or in communities that allow useful links. Measure ICP-fit joins — not raw signup vanity.

### 3. Workaround and review follow-ups (polite, dated)

People who already described the pain in reviews or “how I hack this with spreadsheets” posts are warmer than cold DMs. Ask what they tried, what “done” looks like, whether your named promise would be worth time. Earnest answers > polite “cool idea.”

### 4. Peer founders who serve adjacent audiences

Not “influencer seeding.” A few builders whose buyers overlap yours — trade intros, co-host a short thread, swap a checklist. Keep it reciprocal and specific. Skip spray affiliate pitches.

### 5. Your existing inbox and ship log

If you already have users, waitlisters, or newsletter readers: ask what almost made them churn, what they tell friends, who else has the same pain. Early customers are a channel when you listen and invite referrals without scripting spam.

## What to skip (for now)

- Hiring a marketer before you can describe the channel and the pass/fail bar yourself
- “Content engines,” agency retainers, or hub dashboards that promise set-and-forget acquisition
- Auto-replies, DM sequences, or engagement pods
- Ads before the promise lands organically with the right people
- Invented volume metrics (“we touch 10k conversations a day”) — you will not have them, and neither should you claim them

Skipping is strategy when your calendar is the bottleneck.

## A weekly rhythm that does not require a hire

Keep it boring and dated:

1. **Monday** — pull fresh evidence / queue posts that might clear the worth-reply bar (research first).
2. **Two or three days** — write manual replies; soft-offer the promise page only when useful.
3. **One block** — update the experiment scorecard (joins, ICP-fit chats, clear nos).
4. **Friday** — kill, keep, or narrow. Do not open a second channel because the first felt quiet for three days.

Silence is data. Panic-posting is not.

## Checklist

Before you spend another evening on “marketing”:

1. Can I name **who** buys and **where** they already talk?
2. Is my promise in **their** language, with one next step?
3. Am I picking **one primary channel** this week — not five?
4. Will every touch be **manual and useful** before any pitch?
5. What number would make me **stop** or rewrite — not “post more”?
6. Only then: show up. No hire required until the bottleneck is clearly time-at-scale, not clarity.

If step 1 or 2 is fuzzy, go back to research. Channels will not fix a vague wedge.

## FAQ

### When *should* I hire marketing help?

When you already know the channel, the promise, and the pass/fail bar — and the bottleneck is hours, not clarity. Hiring to invent positioning for you usually buys slides, not customers.

### Isn’t manual reply too slow to “get customers”?

For first customers, slow and specific beats fast and spammy. Early revenue and learning come from a few right conversations. Scale systems after the promise is proven — not before.

### Can I use tools without becoming an agency product?

Yes. Tools that help you **listen, score, and queue** so *you* reply are different from tools that post as you or spray CTAs. Scoring is not engaging. Engaging stays human.

### How does this connect to a first marketing experiment?

Channels are where the experiment runs. [Design one falsifiable test](/blog/how-to-run-a-first-marketing-experiment-before-you-build), pick one honest channel from this list, and read the result before you hire anyone to “own growth.”
