Research without a test is a notebook. Building without a test is a bet you cannot afford to lose twice.

You already know how to [find app ideas people actually need](/blog/how-to-find-app-ideas-people-need), [research your app audience](/blog/how-to-research-your-app-audience), and tell [when a complaint is a build signal](/blog/when-a-customer-complaint-is-a-build-signal). This post is the next step: **one marketing experiment before you build** — not a launch plan, not ads, not a hire.

Written for **solo app builders**, not agencies.

## What a first marketing experiment is (and is not)

A first marketing experiment answers one question with evidence you can collect this week:

> Will people who match my audience take a small next step when I show up with a specific promise?

It is **not**:

- A full funnel, content calendar, or brand kit
- Paid ads you cannot afford to learn from
- “Post every day” without a hypothesis
- Auto-replies, outreach blasts, or hiring a growth person

It **is** a cheap, dated test: a promise in their language, a place they already hang out, and a clear pass/fail signal.

## Preconditions (skip these and you are guessing)

Before you design the experiment, you should already have:

1. A **recurring problem** with dated evidence — not one viral rant ([build signal bar](/blog/when-a-customer-complaint-is-a-build-signal)).
2. A **named audience** — who speaks, where, with what workaround ([audience research](/blog/how-to-research-your-app-audience)).
3. A **one-line promise** in *their* words (“stops X so you can Y”), not “AI-powered platform.”

If any of those are fuzzy, keep researching. An experiment on invented demand only teaches you how to waste evenings.

## Design one experiment in five lines

Write this on a single page. If it needs a second page, cut it.

1. **Hypothesis** — “Solo founders who already complain about X will click / reply / join when I offer Y.”
2. **Audience slice** — one speaker cluster, not “everyone with the keyword.”
3. **Channel** — one room you can reach without inventing distribution (a subreddit, a thread type, a review reply habit, a waitlist shared in places they already are).
4. **Offer** — the smallest next step: a waitlist that names the pain, a useful manual reply with a soft follow-up, a one-page landing promise, a short checklist PDF. Prefer usefulness before pitch.
5. **Pass / fail** — numbers you can count by Friday (e.g. 5 ICP-fit conversations, 10 waitlist joins from people who match, or clear “not worth it” replies). Silence counts as data.

Pick **one** channel and **one** offer. Parallel “tests” are how solos fake progress.

## Three honest experiments that fit a solo calendar

### 1. Promise page + waitlist

Ship a single page that states the complaint-shaped promise and collects email. Share it only where your audience already talks — or reply manually in threads that clear a [worth-reply bar](/blog/when-a-reddit-or-x-reply-is-worth-your-time) and offer the page as an optional next step. Measure joins and whether joiners sound like your ICP.

### 2. Manual reply thread

Find a handful of fresh, specific asks from people who match your audience. Answer helpfully by hand. Soft-offer a next step only after you were useful. Measure follow-up questions and DMs that want more — not vanity upvotes.

### 3. Workaround interview sprint

Message (politely) people who already described the workaround. Ask what they tried, what “done” looks like, and whether a named promise would be worth time. Measure earnest answers, not polite “cool idea.”

None of these require a marketing hire. All of them beat three months of features nobody asked for.

## How to read the result

- **Pass** — ICP-fit people take the next step or describe urgency. You earned a tiny build or a sharper promise.
- **Fail** — silence, “nice” with no urgency, or interest from people you will never serve. Kill or rewrite the promise; do not “add features” to save it.
- **Messy** — mixed signals. Narrow the audience slice and rerun *one* experiment. Do not open a roadmap.

Either way, you learned cheaper than a repo full of unused screens.

## Checklist

Before you write code for the product:

1. Do I have **dated evidence** of the same pain across independent sources?
2. Can I name **who** has it and **where** they talk?
3. Is my promise in **their language**, with one next step?
4. Is there exactly **one channel** and **one offer** this week?
5. What number or outcome would make me **stop** — not just “learn something”?
6. Only then: build the thinnest thing the experiment actually needs (often: nothing clickable yet).

If step 5 is “I’ll know it when I see it,” you do not have an experiment. You have vibes.

## FAQ

### Do I need a landing page to call it a marketing experiment?

No. A landing page is one instrument. A careful reply thread or five earnest conversations can falsify a promise faster. Use a page when you need a durable URL for the offer — not because templates say so.

### Isn’t “talk to customers” enough?

Talking helps when evidence is thin. A first marketing experiment adds a **public or semi-public next step** and a pass/fail bar so you are not only collecting compliments. Interviews and experiments stack; neither replaces dated complaints.

### Should I run ads for the first test?

Usually no. Ads teach creative and bidding when you already know the promise lands with the right people. For a solo first test, organic rooms plus a clear offer teach more per hour.
